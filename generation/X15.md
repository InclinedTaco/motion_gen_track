# Why We Abandoned X14

X14 inherited X13's planner interface: every future frame carries explicit `vx`, `vy`,
`pelvis_z`, `heading_sin/cos`, mode one-hot, goal mask, and three goal positions — 19
dimensions per frame, 285 dimensions of commands to fabricate at inference.

The model learns the joint distribution of all those signals as they co-occur in the
training data. At inference, the planner has to produce a combination that lies on that
learned manifold. Walking velocity, pelvis height, heading, and goal positions are strongly
correlated in each dataset in specific ways. Any combination that deviates from the training
manifold is out-of-distribution, and the model breaks.

The ETH Zurich motion generation paper conditions on three things only: heading vector +
terrain height scan + 2 past robot state frames. It does not command velocity, pelvis
height, step frequency, or gait phase. The generator decides all of that from the motion
prior.

The same principle applies here. We do not care what velocity the model chooses to walk at
or what height it crouches to. We just need it to walk, pick up, carry, and place.

Two consecutive past state frames already encode velocity (`root_delta_xy`) and crouch
depth (joint state). There is no reason to also command them explicitly. Doing so teaches
the model to expect a consistent (velocity, height, heading, goal) tuple at inference —
a tuple the planner cannot reliably produce.

X15 drops all velocity, pelvis height, and heading commands. It conditions only on past
state (2 frames × 73D) + task phase (4D) + active goal (3D) = 153D total, versus X14's
431D. The planner now needs to produce 7 numbers instead of 285, and all 7 are things the
PPO planner can genuinely read from the scene.
